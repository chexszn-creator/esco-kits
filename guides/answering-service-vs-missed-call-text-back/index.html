Answering service vs missed-call text back for contractors

Updated 2026-10-01 · AutoFlow Digital. Swap the {placeholders} for your own details.

If you're a small trade shop missing calls, there are three common fixes: pay a live answering service, hire or share a receptionist, or set up an automatic text back when a call goes unanswered. They aren't mutually exclusive, and the right answer depends on your call volume, budget and how urgent your calls are.

Side-by-side

  • Live answering service: a person picks up 24/7 and takes a message or books from your calendar. Good for emergency-heavy trades. Usually billed per minute or per call, so cost rises with volume. Quality depends on how well you train them.
  • In-house receptionist / CSR: best customer experience and can actually sell and schedule. Highest fixed cost, and there's still lunch, sick days and after-hours to cover.
  • Missed-call text back: when nobody answers, the caller gets a text within seconds. Very low cost and works with most phone systems or carriers' features. Some callers won't text, and it still needs a person to reply and book.

A simple way to decide

  1. Count a normal week's missed calls (your phone log or carrier portal shows them).
  2. Write down how many were after hours vs during the day.
  3. If most misses are daytime while you're on jobs, text back plus a fast human follow-up is usually enough to start.
  4. If you get real emergencies at night (no heat, burst pipe), consider an answering service for after hours only, with text back as the backup.

Script: text back (works with either setup)

Hi, this is {Business}. Sorry we missed your call! Is this an emergency today or something we can schedule? Reply 1 for urgent, 2 to schedule.

Script: what to tell your answering service

Greeting: "Thanks for calling {Business}, this is {Agent}. Are you calling about a problem happening right now, or to schedule service?"
If urgent: get name, address, callback number, what's happening (no heat / leak / no power), then page on-call tech {OnCallNumber}.
If scheduling: get name, address, number, best days. Tell them: "Someone from our office will text you a time by {time} tomorrow."
Never quote prices. Never promise a same-day arrival time.

The part that matters most

Whichever you pick, the lead is only saved when someone follows up quickly and books it. Put a named person on "missed-call follow-up" every day and check the log at lunch and at close.
