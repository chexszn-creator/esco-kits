Auto repair shop missed-call text back script

Updated 2026-10-01 · AutoFlow Digital. Swap the {placeholders} for your own details.

Your service writer is usually at the counter with a customer, on the phone with a parts supplier, or out in the bay when the next call rings. Auto repair callers want two answers: can you look at it, and when? A text back that collects the vehicle info and offers a drop-off slot often books the job before the customer has finished searching for other shops.

1. First text

Hi, {Shop} here. Sorry we missed your call. We're helping a customer. What's the year, make and model, and what's it doing? (warning light, noise, brakes, A/C, won't start, or a quote for a specific repair)

2. Warning light

Thanks. Is the check engine light steady or flashing? If it's flashing or the car is running rough, it's best not to drive it far. We can scan it {Day}. Drop-off at {Time} work? Or we can recommend a tow.

The flashing-light question is a genuine safety point, and it tells you whether this is a same-day job.

3. Brakes / noise

Got it. Is it squealing, grinding, or does the pedal feel soft? If it's grinding or the pedal goes low, please don't drive it more than you have to. We can inspect it {Day} at {Time}.

4. Price-shopper ("how much for brakes?")

Happy to help. Brake jobs vary by vehicle and what's worn (pads only vs pads and rotors). For a {Year Make Model}, front pads usually run {Range}. We'll confirm with a free inspection before any work. Want a slot {Day}?

Give a range, not a single number, and always tie it to an inspection. A fixed price by text becomes a review complaint when the rotors turn out to be shot.

5. Estimate approval (after diagnosis)

Hi {FirstName}, your {Vehicle} needs: {Item 1} {Price1}, {Item 2} {Price2}. Total {Total} plus tax. Reply YES to approve all, or tell us which items. Happy to call and explain.

6. Ready for pickup

Your {Vehicle} is ready! Total {Total}. We're open until {Time}. Old parts are in the trunk if you'd like to see them.

Tips for shops

  • Get written (text) approval before extra work. It protects you and the customer.
  • Offering to show old parts builds trust and prevents "they upsold me" reviews.
  • Count how many missed calls became repair orders. That's your recovered revenue.
