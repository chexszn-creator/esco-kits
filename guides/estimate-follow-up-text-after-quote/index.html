Estimate follow-up texts for contractors

Updated 2026-09-28 · AutoFlow Digital. Swap the {placeholders} for your own details.

A lot of estimates go quiet. That's often not because the customer said no. They got busy, or they have a question they didn't want to call about. A short follow-up sequence lets them reply easily.

Day 2: helpful check-in

Hi {FirstName}, {Name} from {Business}. Did the estimate for the {Job} make sense? Happy to explain any line item.

Day 7: remove the friction

Hi {FirstName}, just checking in on the {Job}. If timing or budget is the issue, we may have options (phasing the work, a different model, etc.). Want me to look?

Day 14: the polite close-out

Hi {FirstName}, I'll close out the {Job} estimate for now so I'm not bugging you. If you want to move forward later, just reply here and I'll pick it back up.

People often reply to the close-out message, because it tells them the decision is theirs and you're done chasing.

Rules of thumb

  • Stick to three follow-ups. Past that it starts to feel like pressure.
  • Each message should give them something, like an answer, an option or an easy way out.
  • Don't invent deadlines ("price goes up Friday") unless the deadline is real.
