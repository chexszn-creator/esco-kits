Handyman missed-call text back script

Updated 2026-10-01 · AutoFlow Digital. Swap the {placeholders} for your own details.

Handyman calls are all over the place: a door that sticks, three pictures to hang, a fence board, a ceiling fan. The risk isn't only missing the call. It's driving across town for a 15-minute job. A good text back gets the whole job list and photos before you commit, and it sets your minimum without scaring off good customers.

1. First text

Hi, this is {Name} with {Business}. Sorry I missed you, I'm on a job. What do you need done? A list and a few photos are perfect, and I'll reply with a price range and the next opening.

2. Setting your minimum (politely)

Thanks for the list! My minimum visit is {Price} (covers up to {1 hour}). Most people save up a few small jobs so it's worth it. Anything else on your list while I'm there?

Asking "anything else?" often turns a single fan install into a half-day job, which is good value for the customer and for you.

3. Half-day / full-day offer

Based on your list, this looks like about {X} hours. I can book a half day ({Price}) on {Day} morning. Materials are extra at cost, or I can pick them up for you. Want it?

4. Outside your scope

Thanks for thinking of me! {Panel work / gas lines / major plumbing} needs a licensed {electrician/plumber}, so I'd recommend calling one directly. Happy to help with anything else on your list.

Turning down licensed-trade work politely protects you, and customers remember you for the next small job. Check your state's rules on handyman job size and licensing.

5. Booking confirmation

Booked: {Day} {Window} at {Address}. Jobs: {list}. I'll text when I'm on the way. If you can, clear space around each spot. Reply R to reschedule.

6. After the job

All done! Invoice: {Link}. If anything isn't right, text me. If you were happy, a quick Google review helps a small business like mine a lot: {ReviewLink}

Tips

  • Keep a "next time" list for every customer and text them in a few months.
  • Group jobs by neighborhood to cut drive time.
