How fast should a home service business respond to leads?

Updated 2026-09-30 · AutoFlow Digital. Swap the {placeholders} for your own details.

When someone has a broken AC or a leaking pipe, they'll usually go with whoever answers them first and sounds competent. You don't have to reply in five seconds. You do need a reply time you can hit every time, plus a first message you can send quickly.

A realistic target

  • Business hours: the first text within a few minutes of a missed call or web lead. Aim for a minute if you can.
  • After hours: an automatic acknowledgement right away, and a real reply by opening time.

Decide who covers the phone

Most slow responses happen because everyone assumes someone else has it. Write it down:

8a–12p: {Office person}
12p–1p (lunch): {Owner} phone
1p–5p: {Office person}
After 5p / weekends: auto-reply + {On-call} checks at {Times}

The first-response script

Hi, {Business} here. Got your {call/request}. Is this urgent today or something to schedule? Reply 1 or 2.

Once they reply, you can take your time getting the details right. The first message just needs to go out fast so they stop calling around.

Measure it yourself

For two weeks, write down the time each lead arrived, the time of your first reply, and whether it booked. You'll know your own response time and booking rate, which is more useful than any industry average.
