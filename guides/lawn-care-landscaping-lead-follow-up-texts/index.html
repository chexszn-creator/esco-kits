Lawn care and landscaping lead follow-up texts

Updated 2026-10-01 · AutoFlow Digital. Swap the {placeholders} for your own details.

Lawn, landscaping and pest control leads come in waves. Spring hits and everybody calls at once while you're out mowing. These texts keep those leads warm until you can quote them.

1. Missed call

Hi, this is {Business}. Sorry we missed you, we're out on properties. What can we help with: mowing, cleanup, landscaping or something else? Reply with your address and we'll get you a quote.

2. Getting what you need to quote

Thanks! To quote it fast: is it front, back or both? Any gates or dogs we should know about? A couple of photos of the yard help too.

3. Quote sent

Hi {FirstName}, your quote for {service} at {Address} is here: {Link}. It's ${price} per {visit/month}. Reply YES to start and we'll put you on the {Day} route.

4. Quote follow-up (2–3 days later)

Hi {FirstName}, checking in on the {service} quote. Any questions? We have a spot on our {Day} route if you'd like to get started.

5. Last follow-up (about a week later)

Last check from {Business}: should I keep your {service} quote open, or close it for now? Either is fine, just reply KEEP or CLOSE.

Giving people an easy "no" usually gets more replies than a third "just checking in."

6. Seasonal check-in to past customers

Hi {FirstName}, {Business} here. We're booking {fall cleanups / spring aeration} now. Want us to add {Address} to the schedule? Reply YES and we'll text a date. (Reply STOP to opt out.)

Tips

  • Route-based businesses can offer a day ("our Tuesday route") instead of exact times. It's honest and easier to schedule.
  • Ask for photos early. It saves a site visit for small jobs.
  • Only send seasonal texts to customers who've agreed to hear from you.
