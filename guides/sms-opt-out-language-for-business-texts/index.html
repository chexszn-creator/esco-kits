SMS opt-out language for business texts

Updated 2026-10-01 · AutoFlow Digital. Swap the {placeholders} for your own details.

If your shop texts customers (missed-call replies, reminders, review requests), you need a simple way for people to opt out and a habit of respecting it. This page gives copy-paste wording. It isn't legal advice. Rules differ by country and state, and your texting provider may have its own requirements, so check with them and with a lawyer for anything marketing-related.

Opt-out line to add to your first message

Reply STOP to opt out.

Short is fine. Put it on the first text in a conversation and on any message that isn't a direct reply to something the customer asked.

Missed-call text with opt-out

Hi, this is {Business}. Sorry we missed your call. How can we help? (Reply STOP to opt out.)

Consent line for a web form

☐ Text me about my request and appointment. Message frequency varies. Msg & data rates may apply. Reply STOP to opt out.

Keep the box unchecked by default and keep marketing consent separate from appointment consent.

Consent on the phone

"Is it OK if we text this number with your appointment time and updates?"
(Note in the job record: text consent given by phone, {date}, {staff initials}.)

When someone replies STOP

You've been unsubscribed from {Business} texts and won't get more messages. Call {Phone} anytime if you need us.
  • Most texting platforms send a confirmation and block further messages automatically. Check that yours does.
  • Mark the contact in your CRM or job software so nobody texts them by hand later.
  • Don't argue or send "are you sure?" follow-ups.

Business texting registration (US)

In the US, texting from a regular 10-digit business number through an app usually requires A2P 10DLC registration through your provider. Registration asks for your opt-in and opt-out wording, so having the lines above written down makes it faster.
