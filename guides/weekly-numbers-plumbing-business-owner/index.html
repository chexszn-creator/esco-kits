Weekly numbers every plumbing business owner should track

Updated 2026-10-01 · AutoFlow Digital. Swap the {placeholders} for your own details.

You don't need a dashboard with forty charts. Most small plumbing shops can run on seven numbers looked at once a week, written on one page. Pick the same day and time every week, Friday afternoon or Monday at 7am, and keep the old sheets so you can see the trend.

The seven numbers

  1. Bookable calls: how many real service requests came in.
  2. Booking rate: booked jobs ÷ bookable calls.
  3. Missed calls and how many were followed up the same day.
  4. Average ticket: total invoiced ÷ number of completed jobs.
  5. Callbacks / recalls: jobs you had to go back to for free.
  6. New reviews, and your average rating this week.
  7. Accounts receivable: money invoiced but not yet collected, and anything over 30 days.

One-page weekly template

Week of: ________
1. Bookable calls ......... ____  (last wk ____)
2. Booking rate ........... ____% (last wk ____%)
3. Missed / followed up ... ____ / ____
4. Avg ticket ............. $____ (jobs: ____ revenue: $____)
5. Callbacks .............. ____  (which tech/what job?)
6. New reviews ............ ____  avg ★ ____
7. AR total / over 30 days  $____ / $____
One thing to fix next week: _______________________
Who owns it: ________

Reading the numbers

  • Calls up but booking rate down: listen to some calls or review the phone script.
  • Average ticket dropping: check whether techs are offering options (repair vs. replace, add-ons like expansion tanks or shut-off valves) where they genuinely fit.
  • Callbacks rising: look for a pattern by tech or job type before blaming anyone.
  • AR over 30 days growing: tighten collection at the time of service.

The "one thing to fix" line is the most important part. Seven numbers with no action is just paperwork.
