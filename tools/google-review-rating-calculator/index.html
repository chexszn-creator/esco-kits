How the calculation works

Your rating is the average of all your star ratings. To raise it from current rating R on N reviews to target T using only 5-star reviews, you need x new reviews where (R×N + 5x) ÷ (N + x) ≥ T, which gives x = N×(T − R) ÷ (5 − T), rounded up.

Because the rating Google shows is rounded, your true average may be a little above or below it. If you're close to a threshold, aim one or two reviews past the number shown here. Google may also filter some reviews, so the count you see is the one that matters.

How to get there faster

  • Ask at the moment of relief, when the job is done and the customer is happy, with a direct review link by text.
  • Reply to every review. Thoughtful replies (especially to negative ones) show future customers you care, and they encourage more people to leave reviews.
  • Never buy or gate reviews. Google's policies prohibit fake reviews and selectively asking only happy customers.

Frequently asked questions

How many 5-star reviews do I need to go from 4.0 to 4.5?

It depends on your current count. With 40 reviews at 4.0, you need 40 × 0.5 ÷ 0.5 = 40 new 5-star reviews. The calculator does this math for any numbers.

Can I ever reach exactly 5.0 again after a bad review?

Not mathematically. Once you have any review under 5 stars, your average can approach 5.0 but never reach it. That's why the target has to be below 5.0.

Does Google round the rating?

Google shows your average to one decimal place, so your exact average can be slightly different from what's displayed. Aim slightly above the threshold you want to show.

Does replying to reviews raise my rating?

Replies don't change the math, but they show future customers how you treat people, and they often lead unhappy reviewers to update their review after you fix the problem.
