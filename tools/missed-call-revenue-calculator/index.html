How the calculation works

Lost revenue per month = missed calls per week × close rate × average job value × 52 ÷ 12. It assumes a missed caller would have booked at the same rate as the callers you do answer. That's a reasonable starting point, because most people who reach voicemail call the next business on the list instead of leaving a message.

Treat the result as an estimate, not a forecast. Some missed calls are spam, existing customers or vendors. If you know roughly what share are real new leads, lower the "missed calls" number to match.

How to cut the number

  • Text back within 60 seconds. A quick "sorry we missed you, how can we help?" keeps many callers from dialing the next company.
  • Triage in the text. Numbered replies (1 = emergency, 2 = schedule) get answered fastest.
  • Track recovered jobs weekly. Count missed calls that became booked jobs. That's the money the system brings back.

Frequently asked questions

How many missed calls is normal for a small service business?

It varies a lot by trade and season. Owner-operators who are on jobs all day often miss a large share of their calls. The only reliable number is your own, so check your phone or carrier call log for a normal week.

Do missed callers call back?

Some do, but many call the next business in their search results, especially for urgent problems like leaks, no heat or lockouts. That's why the calculator assumes missed calls book at your normal close rate.

What is a missed-call text back?

It's an automatic or quick manual text sent to anyone whose call you didn't answer, so they know you got the call and can reply right away instead of calling a competitor.

Is my data saved?

No. The calculator runs entirely in your browser, and nothing you type is sent anywhere.
